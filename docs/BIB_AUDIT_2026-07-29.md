## How this style behaves — verified by running `bibtex`, not by reading the file

Four mechanical facts about `manuscript/Definitions/mdpi.bst` decide many verdicts below. Each was
confirmed by compiling a probe `.bib` against the actual style and reading the `.bbl`.

| Fact | Evidence | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `format.title` (`:272`) emits `title` verbatim; `change.case$` appears **0 times** in the file | grep | **Every brace finding is cosmetic.** Missing or extra `{}` cannot change output. No entry is graded FIX for braces alone. |
| `format.vol.num.pages` (`:542`) reads `number` only to raise a warning — it **never prints it** | source | Adding `number = {…}` to an `@article` renders nothing. Article numbers go in `pages`. There is no `articleno` field. |
| `FUNCTION {misc}` (`:996`) emits authors, title, `howpublished`, date, eprint, `note`, doi — **no `publisher`, no `url`** | compiled | `publisher = {Zenodo}` is silently discarded. `howpublished` is the correct slot. |
| `FUNCTION {software}` and `FUNCTION {default.type}` **both absent** | grep | `@software` produces **no `\bibitem` at all** — the reference silently vanishes. `@misc` must stay. |

Two further rendering traps, both reproduced:

```
booktitle = {Proceedings of the 34th International Conference on Machine Learning}
  → "In Proceedings of the Proceedings of the 34th International Conference…"   (style adds the prefix)

publisher = {Springer}, address = {Cham, Switzerland}
  → "…ECCV 2018, Cham, Switzerland, 2018"        ← Springer dropped
publisher = {Springer}                            (no address)
  → "…ECCV 2018. Springer, 2018"                 ← Springer kept
```

`address` in this style is the **conference city**, not the publisher's office (the IGARSS entries
already use it that way).

### The four that need rewording before they go in the paper

**1. The IoU sentence.** Maxwell & Warner's IoU is **bounding-box** IoU throughout — *"the proportion
that is shared of the combined area of the reference bounding box and predicted bounding box"*, and
Figure 2's caption reads *"the IoU metric, which is commonly used to assess feature recognition
results using bounding boxes."* Their objection is not about semantic-segmentation mean IoU at all.
This makes the narrative's operational rule (keep the citation away from our mean-IoU definition)
**more** right, but its summary sentence is too flat — it invites a writer to cite them as an
authority against our metric, which the paper does not support. Their actual sentence also hedges:
*"not an appropriate **general** measure."* Write instead: *Maxwell and Warner note that bounding-box
IoU does not incorporate boundary uncertainty and is therefore not an appropriate general accuracy
measure in remote sensing.*

**2. "their intensive class is improved grasslands"** → **"included"**. The source reads: *"The
'intensive' class **included** improved grasslands (both permanent and reseeded), fertilised, with a
low number of grass species or mono-species swards."* The class is *named* "intensive". Mild — the
paper itself coordinates "improved and intensive" elsewhere — but it is a one-word fix.

**3. The hedge-stripped quotation.** The narrative puts *"termed semi-natural grasslands or rough
grazing"* **in quotation marks**, and that string is a verbatim substring of the source with the
hedge sliced off the front: *"...and **may be** termed semi-natural grasslands or rough grazing."*
Truncation that changes claim strength is exactly this project's documented failure mode. Also,
"or rough grazing" comes from the Introduction's description of extensive *management*, whereas the
class-label definition says only *"commonly referred to as semi-natural grasslands"* — do not
attribute "rough grazing" to the class definition.

**4. 55.6% and "three years".** Re-derived from their Table 5: 55.6% is **5 correct out of 9**
extensive fields, in an **n = 59** test set. Quote it as corroborating the *direction* of confusion,
never as a skill level. It belongs specifically to the **weakly-supervised** Sentinel RF (trained on
CNN-predicted labels), not the photograph CNN (extensive PA 86.1%) and not the manually-labelled
LUCAS RF (68.63%). That model also used **April–October only**, a further narrowing beyond the
single-annual-cycle compositing. The source is internally inconsistent on the paired user's accuracy
— text says 62%, Table 5 says 62.5%; prefer the table or avoid it.

### Two smaller cautions

- **Fuller's 25–71%** is an *increase in accuracy from excluding boundary regions*, not a share of
  error located at boundaries — and Maxwell & Warner never say whether it is percentage points or
  relative, so it carries irreducible ambiguity. The source is Fuller et al. **1994**, a ~25 m Landsat
  TM map of Great Britain: a different sensor, era and scale from 0.5 m Irish farmland. Safest is to
  quote only their qualitative sentence, *"Error is generally more prominent near thematic
  boundaries."*
- **Krawczyk** states the taxonomy without attribution (*"We may distinguish three main approaches…"*,
  no citation on the stem or any bullet), but the paper is labelled a **review** and credits prior
  surveys for its ground. Cite as a review statement, not as an origin.
- The narrative's enumerated Maxwell & Warner distances are **incomplete** — there is also a 500 m
  saturation threshold, and a *second, different* 5 m that is a processing **cell size**, not a
  threshold. State the rule as covering every distance in the paper rather than a list.

### One check that came back clean

Reina et al. state the tiled-inference effect in their own voice: *"Tiling hyperparameters, which
include tile size, offset, orientation, and overlap, can cause large variations in the prediction,
particularly around the borders of the segmentation mask."* That citation is safe as the narrative
uses it.
